# Age, graduation, and four-year rule: choices for v0.2

**Status:** Non-normative drafting questions.

1. **Age.** The v0.1 proposal uses UIL's younger-than-19-on-September-1 rule for athletics. Should this be universal across all high-school activities or limited to athletics? TAPPS's constitutional wording is “has not reached 19 years of age prior to September 1”; a September 1 birthday may warrant explicit interoperability treatment.
2. **Disability exception.** What documentation establishes that a disability delayed education? Who decides, and should an exception be limited to a single additional year? UIL has a detailed school-based process that a home-school program cannot simply copy without adaptation.
3. **Graduation.** How should the protocol distinguish early accumulation of sufficient credits from actual completion? When is a parent-issued transcript or diploma final, and how should an outside high-school completion credential be treated?
4. **Postsecondary study.** An associate degree or vocational credential need not end a concurrent parent-run high-school program. What evidence shows the two programs are genuinely distinct?
5. **Competition grade.** What evidence supports the first declaration, and who decides a conflict with earlier school placement? Should a pre-high-school repeat be available only under the same circumstances UIL recognizes, or as an independent protocol option subject to equivalent limits?
6. **Time unit.** The v0.1 proposal says four consecutive school years. UIL and TAPPS describe four consecutive years or calendar years after ninth-grade entry. Define the start and end dates precisely, especially for summer contests.
7. **Exceptions and review.** Specify decision authority, proof, deadlines, appeal, and whether any age or four-year exception is recognized for contests with other organizations.
8. **Transition for prior eighth-grade competition.** Should a student who competed in high-school contests as an eighth grader under a home-school program's rules before the Standard's effective date be allowed to complete the ordinary ninth-through-twelfth-grade sequence, even though that produces five years of high-school competition? Define the cutoff date, required contemporaneous records, disclosure, and final sunset. This proposed grandfathering would not give an additional year to a student who first competed in ninth grade. It would not waive age or non-graduation requirements or override an applicable contest organizer's rules.

Roster, residence, transfer, and recruiting questions remain important but belong in later modules. Academic standing and the § 33.0832 assessment regime belong in a separate academic-conformance option.
