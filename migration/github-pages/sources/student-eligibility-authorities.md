# Student eligibility: current external rules

**Status:** Non-normative source notes, checked September 19, 2026. Recheck the applicable year, division, sport, and host organization's rules before use.

- [UIL 2026–27 Constitution and Contest Rules, Subchapter M](https://www.uiltexas.org/policy/constitution/general/eligibility): § 400 requires a varsity contestant not to be a high-school graduate; § 405 defines graduation and distinguishes early credit accumulation from graduation; § 403 sets the athletic age rule; § 408 addresses the four-year high-school window; § 446 contains the detailed disability-related age exception. These rules govern UIL participation, not every independent home-school contest.
- [UIL guidance on HB 3803 and Texas Education Code § 28.02124](https://www.uiltexas.org/policy/2024-25-policy-info/hb-3803): explains parent-elected repeats through grade eight and states that this change does not alter high-school age or four-year eligibility rules. The protocol's competition-grade declaration is a proposed voluntary mechanism, not a claim that the statute directly governs home-school records.
- [UIL constitutional definitions and objectives](https://www.uiltexas.org/policy/constitution/general/overview) and [membership provisions](https://www.uiltexas.org/policy/membership): place definitions and member qualifications before student-eligibility provisions. The Standard's defined use of *school* does not alter UIL's current membership categories; a new UIL category would require UIL's own action.
- [UIL home-school participation guidance](https://www.uiltexas.org/policy/homeschool-faq): explains non-enrolled participation, residence and opt-out rules, previous athletic participation review, and restrictions on participation after enrollment in public school during the same year. UIL participation at a public school and representation of a separate home-school program raise activity-specific conflicts requiring separate analysis.
- [TAPPS Constitution and Bylaws, January 29, 2026 working copy](https://tapps.biz/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TAPPS-Constitution-By-Laws-01292026-small.pdf): Article V addresses age, the four-year window, graduation, and early varsity participation; §§ 81 and 84 give more detail on graduation and the consecutive-year clock, including home-school programs. The opponent-scheduling provisions near PDF page 70 exclude home-school-team students not yet in high school, graduates, and those more than four years from starting ninth grade. These are TAPPS rules for its members, not automatically the universal protocol.
- [TAPPS 2026–28 approved-opponents guidance](https://tapps.biz/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026-28-Scheduling-Approved-Opponents-2.pdf): distinguishes recognized organizations and addresses contests with home-school groups. Recognition or permission to schedule should not be assumed merely because a program follows this proposed protocol.

The student-eligibility draft borrows some conservative boundaries for interoperability. Its proposed wording remains a separate policy choice unless adopted.
